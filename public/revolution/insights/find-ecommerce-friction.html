Find the ecommerce step worth testing

Traffic, product interest and checkout success each need a different response. Find the step where a change would matter to a shopper.

Wojciech Luszczynski2 min read

Put comparable events in order

Product view, basket addition, checkout start, completed order. I decide how retries and duplicate events count. I segment by device, product family or source where the sample allows it, and I pull the same period for every comparison.

Look for the first useful intervention

A synthetic funnel: 1,200 product viewers, 180 basket additions, 108 checkout starts, 72 orders. The steps convert at 15%, 60% and about 67%. The product-page step is where I would look first. Before I choose an experiment I check whether unclear price, missing information or event quality explains the pattern.

One change. One decision window. One stop rule.

Give the test a decision window

Pick one change, for example shipping information shown earlier. Write down the expected improvement, how long orders need to mature, and guardrails such as refunds or lead quality. If spend moves during the test, record it, so the acquisition mix can be checked when the results arrive.

Share the question, not the export

The marketer, the designer and the analyst get the same short brief: the funnel, the proposed change, the owner and the stop rule. Nobody has to reinterpret a whole analytics export to know the next action.

Start where the drop is

In this example the biggest drop is between the product page and the basket. That is the step to investigate, and nothing else yet.
