Design dashboards for the day an input is late

A dashboard that cannot say when its data stopped is a liability. Design it for the day one input is late.

Wojciech Luszczynski2 min read

Show the state of every source

I log each refresh with its source, the period it covers and the result. The last validated snapshot stays, so a short outage does not erase history. Each figure shows when it was updated and whether its inputs cover the same window.

Protect the derived metric

A fictional example. The cost feed has 600 units of spend and the CRM has 24 leads for the same window. Cost per lead is 25 units. If the CRM is a day behind, I hide that ratio or label it as an earlier snapshot. The spend can still stand on its own, with its timestamp.

If an input is late, say so on the number.

Do not let a label change the mapping

The source mapping gets a stable key and its changes are versioned. I add checks for unmatched fields and sudden category changes. A refresh knows what to do with an invalid feed, and an operator can re-run that one feed without touching the others.

Test against the meeting

Walk through three bad days: late cost, late lead counts and a renamed category. Ask whether someone reading the top row can tell what decision is safe today. Then track incomplete reporting windows, unresolved matching exceptions and the time it takes to reconcile a snapshot.

A shorter decision meeting is a good outcome. Measure it separately.
