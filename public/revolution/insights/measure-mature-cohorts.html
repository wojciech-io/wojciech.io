Measure activation with a mature denominator

A cohort belongs in a rate only after its full window has passed. Write the denominator first.

Wojciech Luszczynski2 min read

Write the denominator first

Define an eligible user and the start of the window. If the clock starts at registration, count only users who have a full seven-day window at the reporting cutoff. Use the same eligibility rule for every variant and acquisition source.

Show the users still in observation

A fictional sample. 120 users have completed the window and 36 activated: a mature rate of 30%. Another 80 are still in observation. Divide 36 by all 200 and you get 18%, a number that means something else. I show the newer users as their own group, so the size of the future denominator is visible.

36 of 200, a different question

36 of 120 is 30%. 36 of 200 is another question.

Match the decision to the horizon

Clicks, setup completion, bookings and repeat use answer different questions. A short window tells me whether the instrumentation works. A later window tells me whether behaviour changed. A visitor is assigned to a variant once and carries it through the outcome events. I check for missing and duplicate events before I judge a test.

Print the counts next to the rate

30% is easier to judge beside 36 of 120 and the cutoff date. If I exclude users, I write down the rule. Growth, Product and Sales then argue from the same basis.

One rule, written down

The same eligibility rule, visible counts and a named cutoff. That is the whole method.
