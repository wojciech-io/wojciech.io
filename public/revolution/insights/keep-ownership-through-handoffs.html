Keep the next action visible through a handoff

A work item travels better when status, owner and next action are separate fields. Split them, and a reviewer sees when one goes missing.

Wojciech Luszczynski2 min read

Give each field one job

Status says where the item is in the process. The owner is who is expected to act. The next action is what that person will do. A card can stay in Review while responsibility moves from a writer to an editor. With separate fields, that move shows.

Write a task someone can finish

For a fictional lead, “Waiting” is a state. “Sales owner to confirm integration scope on Thursday” is a commitment. I add a due date when it carries a decision. If the buyer has to reply first, I name who checks back and when.

“Waiting” is a state. A commitment has a name and a day.

Exceptions come first in the meeting

Overdue actions, unowned items and cards that stayed too long in one stage come first. When I export the board to another team's system, the card identifier, the owner and the next step stay the same, so updates after the meeting can be reconciled.

Measure whether handoffs get better

I count open items without an owner, due items closed on time and repeat requests for context, for the same workflow over successive review periods. And I ask the team to remove any field that does not help them decide what to do next.

Status, owner, next action

Three fields. Each one answers a different question.
