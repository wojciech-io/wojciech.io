Nine index points. Twice the price. And a word on OpenAI's own page that most comparisons leave out.

## Nine points is a real gap

Most of the pairings people argue about are separated by a point or two. Luna and Sonnet 5 are level at 38 on v4.3. Sol against Sonnet 5 is a different kind of comparison: **47 against 38**, on the same version of the same index.

That is two thirds of the gap between Anthropic's own Opus 5 and Sonnet 5, which I wrote up in [Sonnet 5 against Opus 5](/insights/claude-sonnet-5-vs-opus-5/). So if the question is only which model is more capable, it has a clear answer.

The question people are really asking is whether nine points are worth double the bill. That depends on two things the index does not measure.

## The price with an end date

OpenAI's model page for Sol lists $4 per million input tokens and $20 output, and describes that as a 20% reduction in input pricing and a 33% reduction in output pricing. The next sentence: promotional pricing is available **at least through November 21, 2026**.

Run those percentages backwards and they imply a reference price of **$5 / $30**. The page does not say Sol returns to that after 21 November. It commits to a date, not to a price, and anything past that date is unknown.

Anthropic's Sonnet 5 went through the opposite process. It launched at $2 / $10 described as introductory, with a scheduled rise to $3 / $15 on 1 September. Anthropic cancelled the rise, and the pricing page now says the increase will not occur.

<KeyPoint>
  Of the two prices in this comparison, one is the vendor's standard price and one is promotional until 21 November. That asymmetry matters more than the ratio for anything you build today and run next year.
</KeyPoint>

I am not predicting an increase. I am saying a cost model that only works at $4 / $20 has a date on it, that date is about nine weeks away, and the page's own arithmetic points at $5 / $30 as the undiscounted figure.

## Long prompts double Sol's input price

The second thing sits in the pricing notes.

On Sol, a prompt with more than **272K input tokens** is billed at 2x input and 1.5x output, and the multiplier applies to the whole request, not only the tokens above the line. Past that threshold Sol costs $8 / $30.

Claude does not do that. Anthropic's pricing page says models from 4.6 on include the full 1M context window at standard pricing, and gives the example directly: a 900k-token request is billed at the same per-token rate as a 9k-token request.

So the price ratio depends on prompt length:

- **Under 272K input tokens:** Sol costs 2x Sonnet 5.
- **Over 272K input tokens:** Sol costs 4x Sonnet 5 on input and 3x on output.

If your workload is whole repositories, long contracts, or large retrieval contexts, the second line is your price, and it changes the answer.

## What would change my mind

OpenAI publishing Sol's price after 21 November. If it confirms $4 / $20 as standard, the price-certainty row disappears and Sol's case on normal-length work gets simpler. If it moves to the implied $5 / $30, the ratio to Sonnet 5 goes from 2x to 2.5x input and 3x output.

A cost-per-completed-task comparison for this pair. Artificial Analysis's run costs cover its whole index, not a specific workload, and a stronger model that finishes in fewer steps can close part of a 2x price gap.

A change to either vendor's long-context policy. The 272K line is the biggest single swing factor in this comparison, and it is a pricing decision, not a technical one.

## Questions people asked

### Is GPT-5.6 Sol better than Claude Sonnet 5?

On capability, clearly: 47 against 38 on Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index v4.3. Sonnet 5 is faster and half the price.

### Is GPT-5.6 Sol more expensive than Claude Sonnet 5?

Twice as expensive at list price, $4 / $20 against $2 / $10. Above 272K input tokens Sol bills the whole request at $8 / $30 while Sonnet 5 stays at $2 / $10, and Sol's current price is promotional through at least 21 November 2026.

### Is GPT-5.6 Sol's price going up?

OpenAI has not announced an increase. Its page commits to promotional pricing through at least 21 November 2026 and says nothing about after. Treat it as known until then.

### Should I use GPT-5.6 Sol or Claude Sonnet 5?

Sol for hard work with prompts under 272K tokens. Sonnet 5 for latency-sensitive work, long-context work above 272K, and anything whose economics must hold at a price you cannot see yet.
