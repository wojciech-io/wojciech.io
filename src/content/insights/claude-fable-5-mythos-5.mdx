Anthropic shipped two frontier models today, June 9, 2026: [Claude Fable 5 and Claude Mythos 5](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-fable-5-mythos-5). One is for everyone. One is locked behind a trusted access program. They are the same model underneath.

That last sentence is the whole story, and most of the coverage will miss it. So before the benchmark screenshots take over your feed, here is what actually changed, what is still a vendor claim, and the four tests I am running from tomorrow.

## Two models, one brain

The naming is the tell. Anthropic says fable comes from the Latin *fabula*, "that which is told," close to the Greek *mythos*. Same root, two masks. The difference is not capability. It is what the model is allowed to do.

  items={[

This matters for anyone building on the API. A silent fallback is better UX than a hard refusal, but it means two different models can answer inside one session. If you are running evals or agent loops, you want to know which one replied.

On frontier physics research, one partner says Fable 5 got "nearly to where GPT-5.5 landed after four days" in 36 hours. If the speed holds, the token efficiency is the part operators should care about. Cheaper reasoning per unit of progress changes what is worth automating.

The software engineering claim is the loudest: Stripe ran a codebase-wide migration across 50 million lines of Ruby in a single day, work Anthropic says would otherwise take a team two months by hand. If even half of that holds on a normal codebase, the build-versus-buy math on internal tooling moves again.

<PullQuote source="Michael Truell, Cursor" role="as quoted in Anthropic's launch post">

</PullQuote>

GitHub, Replit, Hebbia, Morgan Stanley and Harvey all said versions of the same thing: a clear step up on long, agentic, multi-turn work. That is a lot of aligned partners. It is also exactly who you would expect to be quoted on launch day. Hold both thoughts.

## The safety layer is the actual launch

Strip out the benchmarks and this is a safety release. Anthropic is explicit that Mythos-class models "have reached a threshold where they present significant risks," and the entire structure of Fable versus Mythos exists to manage that.

Three classifiers sit in front of Fable 5:

<Grid cols={3}>
  <KeyPoint title="Cybersecurity" type="warning">
    <p>Blocks offensive cyber and exploitation tasks. Anthropic reports zero successful universal jailbreaks across 1,000+ hours of external red-teaming, and says outside partners rated these the most robust cyber safeguards of any model they have tested.</p>
  </KeyPoint>
  <KeyPoint title="Biology and chemistry" type="warning">
    <p>Blocks bioweapon-related and dual-use queries. Deliberately broad for now, which means more false positives. Anthropic says it plans to narrow it as confidence grows.</p>
  </KeyPoint>
  <KeyPoint title="Distillation" type="warning">
    <p>Blocks attempts to extract the model's capability for training a competitor. A capability-proliferation control, not a user-safety one.</p>
  </KeyPoint>
</Grid>

Paired with that is a new data policy: 30-day retention on all Mythos-class traffic, not used to train new models, with all human access logged and deletion after 30 days in nearly all cases. The stated reason is detection: catch novel attacks and tune out false positives before they scale.

<Callout type="note" title="The honest trade-off Anthropic names itself">
  The safeguards are tuned cautious, and Anthropic admits they are "still stricter than would be ideal" and will frustrate some users with false positives. The goal is not zero jailbreaks. It is to make any jailbreak slow and costly enough to catch before it is used at scale. That is a defensible position, stated plainly. I respect that more than a "perfectly safe" claim.
</Callout>

And then there is **Project Glasswing**: the US government collaboration, running since April 2026, where Mythos-class models with cyber safeguards lifted are handed to a small group of cyberdefenders and critical infrastructure providers. Glasswing partners get upgraded from Mythos Preview to Mythos 5 now. A biology trusted access program is coming for select researchers, with the bio safeguards removed and cyber safeguards kept on.

This is the line that should make you sit up: the same query that is defensive in a vetted cyberdefender's hands is an attack in someone else's. Anthropic's answer is not a smarter filter. It is access control. Who you are now decides which model you get.
