Nordic enterprise buyers are not American enterprise buyers with a Swedish accent. The differences are meaningful and if you run a US-playbook motion into Sweden, Denmark, or Finland, you'll get results that look like market rejection but are actually playbook mismatch.

This is a breakdown of the GTM architecture I built for a B2B SaaS expansion into the Nordics — what worked, what didn't, and the specific decisions that drove outcomes.

## Why the Nordic market is different

**Reference culture.** Nordic enterprise buyers talk to each other. A reference from one Swedish procurement head to another carries more weight than any case study you'll ever publish. Getting the first 2-3 reference customers in-market is the entire game for the first 12 months.

**Long buying cycles, low urgency signals.** The typical Nordic enterprise deal takes longer than a comparable UK or German deal. Not because buyers are indecisive — because the procurement process is more committee-driven and consensus-oriented. You'll get positive signals that don't convert because the champion isn't the decision-maker and the decision-maker hasn't been engaged.

**Relationship before vendor.** Cold outbound that leads with product features performs poorly. Outbound that leads with a relevant perspective on a problem they're known to be working on performs significantly better. The first message needs to demonstrate that you've done your homework.

## ICP definition

We spent the first three weeks doing nothing but ICP refinement. The temptation is to start broad and narrow. We started narrow and went narrower.

Final ICP: Nordic-headquartered B2B companies, 200–1,500 employees, in one of four verticals (logistics, fintech-adjacent, HR tech, industrial SaaS), with an active international expansion initiative or recent Series B/C funding. Decision-maker title: CRO, VP Sales, or CMO. Influencer title: Head of Sales Enablement or Revenue Ops.

This sounds restrictive. In practice, the addressable market at this definition was ~400 companies. That's the right number for an outbound motion — it means you can research every account properly.

## Outbound stack

- **Sourcing:** LinkedIn Sales Navigator + manual research layer for company signals (recent hires, job postings, funding announcements)
- **Enrichment:** Clearbit for firmographic data, manual check on Nordic-specific signals
- **Sequencing:** Outreach with custom sequences per vertical, 8-touch, 6-week window
- **Localization:** LinkedIn connection requests in local language; first email in English, follow-ups available in Swedish if the prospect engaged in Swedish

The 8-touch, 6-week sequence was deliberately slower than a standard US playbook. Nordic buyers respond poorly to high-frequency follow-up. The sequence spaced touches at: day 1, day 5, day 12, day 19, day 28, day 38, day 46, day 52.

## What drove meetings

The single highest-performing touchpoint was a personalized voice note on LinkedIn — 45-60 seconds, referencing something specific about the company, asking one clear question. Open rate was unmeasurable; response rate was the highest of any touch type we used.

The lowest-performing touchpoint was a generic "just following up" email. (Obvious in retrospect. Should have cut it earlier.)

## Results shape

First 90 days: 0 meetings (expected — relationship market, long ramp).
Days 90–180: First 6 meetings, 2 from referrals via the first conversations.
Days 180–270: Pipeline established, first deal in final stages.

The pattern is: the first 90 days are investment, not output. Teams that measure the Nordics motion at 60 days will kill it before it works.

## The architecture principle

GTM architecture is the right mental model for this kind of work. You're not running a campaign — you're designing a system with inputs, processing logic, feedback loops, and outputs. The signal processing (who to contact, in what order, with what message) is as important as the outreach mechanics.

Treat it like system design, not campaign management.
