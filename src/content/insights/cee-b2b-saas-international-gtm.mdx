A B2B SaaS company in Warsaw builds something good and sells it well at home. Then it decides to go international. The plan is usually a list of countries and a hiring roadmap.

The obstacle is not on that list. A buyer in Chicago has no mental category for a vendor from Central Europe. Not a negative one, an absent one. Everything you claim gets quietly discounted until something local vouches for you, and no amount of outbound volume fixes an absent reference point.

I ran this as Global Head of Growth at [SentiOne](https://sentione.com) for four years, across the EU, the UK, the US, LATAM and account-based work in the UAE. New customers finished up 80%, brand recognition up 60%, organic traffic up 50%.

<Callout type="warning" title="The discount nobody puts in the plan">
A vendor from an unfamiliar region pays a credibility tax on every first conversation. You can either design around it or spend three years paying it in longer cycles and lower win rates.
</Callout>

## Five markets, one cadence

The instinct is to build a team per country. It feels like respect for local difference. In practice it produces five strategies, five sets of messaging, five reporting formats, and nothing that compounds. Market three learns nothing from market one because there is no shared surface for the lesson to live on.

What worked was the opposite shape. One operating cadence across all markets: the same weekly rhythm, the same pipeline definitions, the same experiment log. Local adaptation happened at three layers and nowhere else.

## The three layers that have to be local

**Proof.** A German enterprise buyer wants a German reference. This is the layer where the credibility discount is actually paid down, and it is why the first customer in a market matters more than the next ten. Everything else is easier once someone local has said yes on the record.

**The language of the problem.** This is a deeper job than translation. The same product solves a problem that different markets describe differently, and buyers search for their own description of it. This is where category content and analyst education did the work at SentiOne: being present in the vocabulary the market already uses.

**The buying process.** Procurement in a Gulf enterprise, a US mid-market deal and a Nordic public tender are three different machines. The material has to fit each one, even when the pitch behind it is identical.

Everything outside those three stays shared. Positioning, product narrative, pricing logic, reporting definitions and the weekly rhythm. The moment those fragment, the markets stop being comparable, and comparability is the only advantage a small international team has.

## The order it went in

1. **Fix the shared layer first.** Positioning, narrative and reporting definitions before any market opens. Retrofitting these across five regions costs several times what setting them once does.
2. **Win one reference per market before scaling spend.** The first local customer is a positioning asset, not just revenue.
3. **Enter the vocabulary, not just the search results.** Category content and analyst relations move the reference point in a way performance marketing cannot.
4. **Keep one experiment log across all regions.** A landing page or offer test that works in one market is a candidate everywhere. That shared log is where the 20% sales lift from offer experimentation came from.
5. **Use ABM where the market is relationship-shaped.** The Gulf work was account-based because the buying process there rewards depth over reach. That decision belongs to the buying-process layer and leaves the strategy untouched.
6. **Report every market the same way, weekly.** Different numbers, identical definitions.

The fourth line is the one worth defending in a budget conversation. An operation that transfers eight experiments a quarter between regions is compounding. One that transfers none is five companies wearing the same logo.

If you are planning an international move out of Central Europe, the first question is not which market. It is who in that market will say your name out loud, and what it takes to get them.
