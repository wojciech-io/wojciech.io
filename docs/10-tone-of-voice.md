# 10 — Tone of voice

The voice for all wojciech.io copy (EN, PL, IT). Single source of truth.
When writing or reviewing copy, write to this.

## In one line

A confident operator who has seen the inside: diagnoses sharply, speaks
in the first person and candidly, and promises to stay until the system works.

## The reference mix

The voice is a deliberate blend of three writers:

- **Kieran Flanagan** — provocative diagnosis, framework clarity, confident
  declaratives. Challenges a comfortable assumption in the first line.
- **Michał Sadowski** — first-person candour, lived experience, warmth,
  build-in-public honesty. Trust through telling it straight.
- **Paweł Tkaczyk** — concrete, vivid contrast and clean images. Makes the
  abstract tangible instead of describing it with adjectives.

Chosen blend: **B+A** — Sadowski's candid, personal base with Flanagan's
provocative operator edge. Tkaczyk's concreteness runs through both.

## Principles — do

- **Open by challenging a comfortable assumption.** "Most B2B companies don't
  have a traffic problem — they have a system problem."
- **First person.** "I build", "I've seen", "I stay". This is one operator,
  not an agency.
- **Concrete contrast over adjectives.** "AI in the deck, Excel in the actual
  work" beats "inconsistent AI adoption".
- **Short declarative sentences.** Vary the rhythm. Let one line land.
- **Name the operator promise.** Scope, build, then stay until it runs.
- **Substance.** Name the actual systems — GTM, CRM, automation, AI — not
  vague "growth".

## Anti-patterns — don't

- No SaaS-brochure adjectives: cutting-edge, innovative, synergy, leverage,
  best-in-class, seamless.
- No consultant hedging: "we could explore", "potentially", "it depends".
- No hype or superlatives without proof.
- No invented metrics — unknown numbers stay TBD.
- Not cold or corporate; not over-casual either. Editorial and human.

## PL / IT

Same voice — never a literal translation. Polish especially must read as
natural, idiomatic copy with the concrete-contrast style intact
(e.g. "Z AI w prezentacjach i Excelem w pracy"). Avoid translationese.

## Canonical example — homepage hero

**Before (old, literal):**
> I build revenue systems that compound.
> Most B2B companies don't have a traffic problem. They have a system problem.
> I build the system: GTM, marketing, CRM, automation, and AI as one operating
> model, not a set of separate campaigns.

**After (B+A voice):**
> I build revenue systems. Then I stay until they work.
> Most B2B companies don't have a traffic problem — they have a system problem.
> Fifteen years inside growth teams showed me the same gap everywhere: great
> traffic with a leaky CRM, AI in the deck but Excel in the actual work. I
> rebuild it as one operating model — GTM, CRM, automation and AI as a single
> machine — and I don't leave when it gets hard.
